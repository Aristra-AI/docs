Aristra remembers what you tell it about yourself, how you like it to work, and what you are working on, so you don't have to say it again. Your memory is kept in your account, so it reaches your chats on the web, your phone, Aristra Desktop and the terminal. Every account has it, with or without a plan, and it costs nothing: a chat that uses your memory pays only for its own tokens.

## Your profile

Your name, your occupation and the **About you** note on your account's **Profile** page start every chat, whatever the Memory switch says. You typed them, so they are a setting, not something Aristra learned.

## What Aristra saves as you chat

When you tell Aristra something worth keeping (a correction, a lasting preference, a fact about you, a decision, or "remember this"), it saves it right away and says so in the chat. It updates or forgets a memory when you correct it or ask. It doesn't save secrets or sensitive details, or anything a web page or file told it to keep. Chats with other people in them write no memory.

You can ask too: "remember that I prefer short answers", "forget where I work".

## Learning from your past chats

Each night Aristra reads your chats in your account since its last look, and keeps what will matter later: a fact about you, how you like answers, a decision or a plan. It keeps at most 15 new memories a night. Each one quotes what you said and names the chat it came from, and a newer one can replace an older memory that it changed.

It also rewrites the summary of you that starts every chat, with what you are working on, open loops, and recent decisions and topics.

- The first night, it reads the last 30 days.
- It needs both **Memory** and **Search and reference past chats** on, and reads nothing while either is off. Once both are back on, it reads the chats from that time too.
- It never reads incognito chats, chats with other people in them, scheduled tasks' own chats, deleted chats, or chats you started with Memory off. Chats kept only on your computer aren't in your account, so it doesn't read them either.
- The chats go to Anthropic's batch service to be read. Aristra deletes each night's batch there once its results are saved; Anthropic never keeps one longer than 29 days. It costs you nothing.

## Turn memory off

Your account has two switches, under **Personalization** on the website and under **Settings › Memory** in Aristra Desktop:

- **Memory**: while it is off, nothing is used or saved. Once it is back on, Aristra may learn from chats still in your history.
- **Search and reference past chats**: lets Aristra look up your earlier chats when you ask about them.

A chat records the Memory switch when it starts. Turning Memory off reaches every chat at once; turning it back on reaches chats you start afterwards.

## Incognito chats

An incognito chat is never learned from and saves nothing to your memory. On the web and in Aristra Desktop's Chat tab, it can still use what Aristra already remembers. In [Aristra Code](/code/guides/memory), an incognito chat neither reads nor saves your memory. See [Chats](/chats#incognito-chats).

## Read and change your memory

Open **Memory** in the website's sidebar (in Aristra Desktop, **Settings › Memory › See and edit your memory**):

| Tab | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Summary** | The summary of you that starts every chat. You can edit it |
| **Memories** | Everything Aristra remembers. **Learned from your chats** at the top lists what it learned each night, each linked to its chat, with **Forget** |
| **Pages** | Your memories grouped by project, repository, folder and topic |
| **Profile** | Your profile |

## Import

**Import** on the Memory page brings in what another assistant remembers about you. It gives you a prompt to paste into ChatGPT, Claude or another assistant; paste or upload what it answers. Up to 500 memories a day. Imported memories reach the summary the next night.

In Aristra Desktop, **Settings › Memory** can also import what Codex and Claude Code remember on your Mac, and keep a copy of your memory as files in `~/Aristra/memory`.

## Memory in Aristra Code

Aristra Code uses the same account memory, and also keeps notes about each repository and reads your `AGENTS.md` files. See [Instructions and memory](/code/guides/memory).
