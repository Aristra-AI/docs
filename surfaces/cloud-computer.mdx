A cloud computer is your own Linux machine in Aristra's cloud, running the same Aristra Code agent as your terminal and Aristra Desktop. Chats on it work like chats on your Mac: a real shell, files that persist, tools you install, long jobs left running. Its system is Ubuntu, and you have root on it. It is always reachable from the web and your phone, and from Telegram with a plan, because it wakes when you send it something.

<Note>
A cloud computer is different from a [cloud chat](/code/cloud-chats). A cloud chat runs on Aristra's shared cloud agent and keeps nothing between chats but your account. A cloud computer is a machine of your own, with a disk that keeps your files and installed tools.
</Note>

## Set one up

A cloud computer runs on your balance: with a [plan](/account/plans), or on [top-ups](/account/credits) without one. With a plan, any balance above zero is enough to make one. Without a plan, you need at least $2 from top-ups; the starter credit doesn't count. Your plan sets the largest size and disk you can have; without a plan, up to Large with a 100 GB disk.

1. Open **Cloud computer** in your account, at `app.aristralabs.com/account/computer`. It is the one place to make, use and manage it.
2. Pick a size, a disk and where it keeps its data (below), then choose **Create cloud computer**.
3. It is ready in a few minutes, and the page shows each step. Start a chat on it from its page, or from **Where it runs** on a new chat.

| Size | Machine | While awake | Available |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 2 shared CPUs, 1 GB memory | $0.0122 an hour | Every plan, and on top-ups |
| Medium (the default) | 4 shared CPUs, 2 GB memory | $0.0243 an hour | Every plan, and on top-ups |
| Large | 8 shared CPUs, 4 GB memory | $0.0487 an hour | Every plan, and on top-ups |
| Extra large | 8 shared CPUs, 8 GB memory | $0.0881 an hour | Max |

Medium is enough for most agent work. Small suits messages, scripts and scheduled tasks; builds and a browser want Large. A new size takes effect at once when it is awake: the computer restarts in a few seconds with everything on its disk. If it is asleep, it wakes with the new size.

**The disk** is chosen when you make it, from 5 GB (10 GB unless you change it), in steps of 5 GB, and can grow up to your plan's limit: 25 GB on Plus, 100 GB on Pro and Max, and 100 GB on top-ups. It never shrinks. It costs $0.18 a month for each GB, whether the computer is awake or asleep, because it keeps your data.

Model calls and tools are charged as they are anywhere else in Aristra.

## Its system

A cloud computer runs Ubuntu 24.04, and the system is yours to change. You and Aristra work in it as `user`, with `sudo` and no password: install packages with `apt`, run servers, change its settings. Node 24 comes installed.

Its disk keeps two things apart:

- **Your home folder**, `/home/user`. Chats start in `/home/user/workspace`. Nothing below changes it.
- **The system**: everything outside your home folder, such as the packages you install.

**System** on its page looks after the second. While the computer sleeps, **Show its system** wakes it for a moment.

| | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Save a checkpoint** | Saves the system as it is now, under a name if you give one. The newest three are kept. |
| **Go back to this** | Puts the system back as it was at that checkpoint. What was installed or changed in the system since then is lost. |
| **Security updates** | Ubuntu's security fixes install about once a week, while the computer is idle. With the switch off, the system gets none unless you install them yourself. |
| **Download files** | Downloads your home folder and the lists of what is installed, as one archive. Keep code in git as well: a push is the quickest copy. |
| **Reset the system** | Gives the computer a fresh system. Everything installed on it and every checkpoint is lost. |

Saving a checkpoint, going back to one and a reset each restart the system, which stops every command running on the computer. None of them touches your home folder.

Programs in the system reach the internet. They can't reach private networks or the ports mail servers use (25, 465, 587 and 2525), so send mail through a provider's API.

## Files and the terminal

Two more cards on its page let you work on the computer yourself, without a chat. Both act as `user`, as Aristra does, and both wake a sleeping computer.

### Files

**Files** shows one folder at a time, starting at your home folder. Open a folder, download a file, upload files into the folder you're in, make a folder, rename or delete.

- Up to 500 MB a file. An upload appears only once all of it has arrived, and replacing a file there asks first.
- A folder your user can't read says so. For the system's own files, use the terminal and `sudo`.
- Files pass through Aristra on their way, and are not kept there.
- While the computer sleeps, **Show its files** wakes it for a moment.

### Terminal

**Open terminal** gives you a shell on the computer, as `user`; `sudo` makes you root. It suits what doesn't belong in a chat:

- Signing in to a tool, such as `gh auth login`, or putting a key in a file without writing it in a chat.
- Programs you drive yourself, such as `psql`, `htop`, `vim` or a log you follow.
- A quick command, with no model's time spent on it.

What you type goes to the computer and back, and Aristra keeps none of it. Only you can open it, from the website.

- **One at a time.** Opening it in another tab closes the first.
- **It keeps the computer awake** while it is open, and you pay for that time. It closes after 30 minutes with nothing typed or shown.
- **Closing it ends what runs in it**, as closing a terminal window does. To keep something running, start it with `nohup` or in `tmux`, or ask Aristra to run it as an [app](#apps-with-an-address).
- A stopped computer has to be started first.

## Where it keeps its data

You choose when you create it, and can change it on its page. A change applies to new messages.

| | Saved to your account (default) | Kept on the computer only |
|---|---|---|
| Chats | On the computer, and each turn also saved to your account | On the computer only |
| Memory | Your account's memory, shared with your Mac and the website | Its own memory, in files on its disk |
| Your account's files, Craft, connectors | Your files and Craft; connectors with a plan | Not used; tools you install on it work |
| On the web and phone | Your chat list shows them, even while it sleeps | Read from the computer when you open them, which wakes it |
| Prompts it schedules itself | Kept on the computer; replies saved to your account | Kept on the computer; replies stay on it |
| Scheduled tasks from the website | The task is in your account; the chat each run starts is saved there too | The task is in your account; the chat each run starts stays on the computer |
| Its own scheduled tasks, on its page | Kept on the computer; the chat each run starts is saved to your account | Kept on the computer; the chat each run starts stays on it |
| Telegram | Each Telegram chat has a chat on the computer, saved to your account | Each Telegram chat has a chat on the computer, kept only there |
| If you delete it | Chats and memory stay in your account | Everything on it is gone |

With **Kept on the computer only**, Aristra passes your messages and its replies through but keeps nothing of them: no saved messages, titles or search entries. A message waits on Aristra's servers only until the computer picks it up, and a reply for at most an hour after it finishes, so an open page can catch up. Its disk is the only copy, so use **Export chats and memory** on its page before you delete it.

In both modes Aristra's servers handle your sign-in, your balance, calls to Aristra's models (counted for billing, their content not kept), web search and other built-in tools, Telegram, and starting and stopping the computer. A scheduled task you make on the website lives in your account, because the website runs it.

When you switch to **Kept on the computer only**, the page offers to delete the copies of its chats already in your account. When you switch back, it offers to upload the chats on the computer.

## Sleeping and waking

- **It sleeps after two minutes idle**: no reply running, no approval waiting, no background command, nothing from you, no visit to one of its [apps](#apps-with-an-address), and no [terminal](#terminal) open. Anything it can't check counts as busy, so it never sleeps in the middle of work.
- **It wakes when you send it something**: a message from the web, your phone or Telegram, or an approval. A message sent while it sleeps waits and is answered once it is up, usually within seconds. Small, Medium and Large keep what was running in memory while asleep; Extra large shuts down instead and starts fresh, so it takes a little longer to wake. Your files and chats are on its disk either way.
- **A visit to one of its apps wakes it too.** The first page after a sleep takes about a second. An Extra large takes longer, because it starts fresh.
- **Scheduled prompts still run on time.** The computer keeps them on its disk and tells Aristra only when the next one is due, so Aristra can start it just before. Aristra never learns what the prompts say.
- While it sleeps you pay only for its disk.
- If it stops for another reason (a crash, maintenance on its host), it restarts by itself, or its next scheduled prompt or your next message starts it.

## Apps with an address

Ask Aristra to run something you built on the computer: a web app, an API, a bot, anything that listens on a port. Aristra runs it as an app:

- **It keeps running.** The computer starts it again if it ends, and after the computer restarts. It outlives the chat that made it.
- **It gets an address** under `aristra.app` that only you can open, in a browser signed in to Aristra, until you share it with people by email, as you [share a site](/sites#share-it-with-people), or, with a plan, [make it public](/sites#make-it-public).
- **It can answer at a domain of yours**, as a [site can](/sites#a-domain-of-your-own).
- **It is told who is asking.** Each request reaches the app with the visitor's email address in the `X-Aristra-Email` header, yours or that of someone you shared it with, so the app needs no sign-in of its own. A public app is told nobody's: its requests come without that header.
- **A visit wakes the computer.** While it sleeps, the first page takes about a second (longer on an Extra large).

**Apps** in your account, at `app.aristralabs.com/account/apps`, lists your apps and their addresses. **Take down** ends an app's addresses: they stop working and are not given out again. The app itself keeps running until Aristra stops it, so ask Aristra in a chat on the computer to stop it. A computer runs up to eight apps.

Aristra can also publish a folder of web files as a site at an address of the same kind, with nothing left running. See [Sites](/sites).

### A public app

A public app works like a [public site](/sites#make-it-public): anyone with the address can open it, at a name of your own if you like. Its HTML pages carry a Report link, and while your account is new, a browser opening it sees a notice first. A few things differ because it is a program:

- **Any site can call it.** A request that another site's page makes reaches the app, without cookies, so a public app can serve an API to other sites; it sends its own CORS headers. The notice is for pages a browser opens, not for calls that ask for data. A private or shared app refuses such requests.
- **Its cookies are renamed.** Each cookie the app sets reaches the browser as `__Host-` and its name, with `Path=/` and no `Domain`, and comes back to the app under the name it gave it. Only those cookies reach the app, so a page at another `aristra.app` address can't plant a cookie on it. Code in the page that reads `document.cookie` sees the renamed cookies.
- **Every visit wakes the computer**, and keeps it awake for two minutes after. An app many people visit can keep the computer awake for much of the day, and you pay for the time it is awake.

### Always on

Ask Aristra to set an app to always on when it must answer without that wait, or work while nobody visits it, as a bot does. The computer then does not sleep while the app exists, and its running time is charged for every hour. **Apps** shows about what that comes to in a month.

### What Aristra asks first

When Aristra asks before it starts or stops an app, **Allow** covers that one time. The answer that begins **Allow, and don't ask again for running and stopping apps** covers every later start, restart and stop in that folder.

Turning always on on is always asked, whatever the chat's mode and whatever else you allowed, because it changes what you pay. That question has its own **don't ask again** answer, which covers always on too.

If an app keeps ending as soon as it starts, Aristra tells you once: on Telegram if you linked it, otherwise by email.

## Use it from anywhere

- **The web and your phone:** on a new chat, **Where it runs** lists Aristra Cloud, your cloud computer and your other computers; choose the cloud computer to open its page in your account, and send a message there. With **Saved to your account**, the chat opens on the usual chat page; otherwise on the cloud computer's own page.
- **Telegram** (with a plan): turn on **Telegram messages go to this computer** on its page. Your private chat with Aristra then runs on it. Send `/new` to start a fresh chat there.
- **Scheduled tasks** (with a plan): a task set to run on your cloud computer starts a chat there with its prompt each time it runs, waking it if it sleeps. The task's own history shows that each run started; the reply is in the chat.
- **Its own scheduled tasks:** under **Scheduled tasks** on its page, give it a prompt to run every day, on weekdays, one day a week, every hour or once, at times in your own time zone. The computer keeps them, wakes for each one, and runs it in a new chat, which **Read it** opens. They need no plan: each run is paid from your balance, as its chats are. Reading the list while it sleeps wakes it for a moment.
- **Aristra Desktop:** the chats of a cloud computer that saves to your account show in the Code tab under **On other devices**. To start a chat on it, use its page on the web.

## Webhooks

A webhook is an address that starts a chat on your cloud computer with each request it receives, such as an event from GitHub or Stripe.

1. Under **Webhooks** on its page, choose **New webhook**.
2. Give it a name, say what Aristra does with each request, and choose how its chats run.
3. Choose **Make the webhook**, and copy its address: it is shown once.

Give the address to the service that sends the events. Each request starts a chat, with your instruction first and the request after it. Aristra is told that the request is data from outside, never instructions to follow.

| Mode | What its chats do |
|---|---|
| **Asks first** (the default) | Ask you before running a command or changing a file, on the web and Telegram |
| **Accepts edits** | Change files without asking, and ask before running a command |
| **Auto** | Run without asking you first. A request comes from outside and may try to steer Aristra, so choose it only for a sender you trust |

- **Anyone with the address can start chats**, so keep it secret. **New address** makes another, and the old one stops working at once. **Pause** refuses requests until you resume it.
- **A retried request is one chat** when its sender names each delivery, as GitHub, Stripe and many others do. Otherwise each request is a chat.
- A request wakes the computer if it sleeps, and its chat is paid for as any chat on it.
- Up to 10 webhooks a computer, a request of up to 256 KB, and up to 30 requests a minute and 500 a day for each.
- The card shows how each webhook's last request went.

## Approvals

When Aristra on your cloud computer asks before a step, the question shows in the chat, on the web and your phone. The computer stays awake while it waits.

- **Allow** covers that one step.
- An answer that begins **Allow, and don't ask again** also saves what it names on the computer, so Aristra doesn't ask about it again.
- Your sign-in is all it takes to answer, or to change the chat's mode. A chat on a [computer of your own](/code/remote-control) asks for a fresh sign-in check before you allow a step from another device. A cloud computer doesn't, because the web is where you work with it.

## Its own model

By default a cloud computer uses Aristra's models, paid from your balance. It can also run on **your ChatGPT plan**:

1. On the cloud computer's page, under **Your own model**, choose **Set up…**.
2. The computer starts its own sign-in and the page shows a code. Open OpenAI's page and enter the code.
3. The computer finishes the sign-in itself and keeps it on its own disk.

Aristra's servers pass the code along and never see the sign-in. Aristra's models stay available from your balance.

## Paying for it

- **While awake**, running time is charged by the second from your plan's monthly usage when you have a plan, then from your top-up balance.
- **The disk** is charged whether the computer is awake or asleep.
- **An app set to always on** keeps the computer awake, so running time is charged for every hour while the app exists.
- When your balance is low, you get an email (at most once a day).
- **With a plan, at a zero balance it goes to sleep.** Nothing on it is deleted while you have a plan. It wakes by itself for your next message once your balance is back, from a [top-up](/account/credits) or your plan's monthly reset. The disk's charge for that time is taken from the next money that arrives.
- **Without a plan, when your top-up balance runs out** it is stopped, and its disk is kept for 30 days. You get an email a week before the disk is deleted. Top up within that time to bring it back with everything on it, or export its data first.
- **When your plan ends** it keeps running from your top-up balance, and an Extra large one drops to Large. With none left it is stopped and its disk kept for 30 days in the same way; renew or top up within that time to bring it back.
- **If you move to a plan with a smaller limit**, its size drops to that plan's largest at your renewal. Its disk stays as it is, and can no longer grow.

## Security

- Each cloud computer is its own machine on its own private network: it can't reach other users' machines or Aristra's servers except through the public API, as your Mac does.
- The machine has two sides. One is your Ubuntu system, where you and Aristra work with root. The other holds its sign-in, its keys, and its chats, memory and settings. No program in your system can read or change that side, root included.
- It has one door, for your apps' addresses and your terminal. A request comes through only after Aristra has checked that it comes from you, or from someone you shared the app with, unless you made the app public. The terminal is yours alone. Your apps listen inside your system and are reached no other way.
- For everything else it connects out to your account over HTTPS, authenticated with its own device key, as your Mac does.
- It holds no credentials of Aristra's. It signs in with a one-time token when it is first made, and with a new one if it has slept for weeks or moves to another host.
